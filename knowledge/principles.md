# The Damodaran Canon — how he thinks about value

Distilled from his blog *Musings on Markets* (683 posts, 2008–Oct 2026), mirrored on his
Substack, plus the frameworks in his books (*Investment Valuation*, *The Dark Side of
Valuation*, *Narrative and Numbers*, *The Little Book of Valuation*, *The Corporate Life
Cycle*, *Investment Philosophies*). Everything here is paraphrased, not quoted. Post
citations use the blog path `YYYY/MM/slug` (prefix `https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/`).
Where his views changed over time, the newer view is the one used here.

---

## I. Value versus price

1. **Value and price are different things, set by different processes.** Value is driven by
   cash flows, growth, and risk. Price is set by demand and supply, mood and momentum. You can
   *value* a company (intrinsic) or *price* it (relative to what others pay). Most "valuations"
   in practice are pricings. Know which game you're playing. *(2020/03/a-viral-market-meltdown-iii-pricing-or, 2016/10/venture-capital-it-is-pricing-not-value)*
2. **Investing is a bet that price converges to value.** That requires a gap and a catalyst.
   Traders play the price game; investors play the value game. Both can make money, but mixing
   them up loses it. *(2014/04/watch-gap-apples-long-and-twisted)*
3. **The market is not always right, but it's a good starting point.** When your value differs
   a lot from the price, first ask what the market sees that you don't. Then decide. *(2013/02/apple-redux-thoughts-on-value-price-and)*
4. **Price-value feedback loops exist.** For some firms (young, capital-hungry, story-driven),
   price changes can change value: access to capital, employee retention, acquisitions with
   stock. *(2021/02/the-price-value-feedback-loop-look-at)*
5. **Good companies can be bad investments and bad companies good ones.** It depends on the price
   paid relative to the expectations already priced in. *(2017/03/explaining-paradox-why-good-bad)*

## II. Stories and numbers

6. **Valuation is a bridge between stories and numbers.** A valuation with numbers but no story
   is a spreadsheet exercise. A story with no numbers is a fairy tale. Every input should trace
   back to a part of the story, and every part of the story should show up in an input.
   *(2014/06/numbers-and-narrative-modeling-story, 2017/01/narrative-and-numbers-how-number)*
7. **The 3P test: possible, plausible, probable.** Possible stories are unlikely but not
   impossible. Plausible ones need evidence that they can happen. Probable ones show up as
   expected values in the numbers. Different stories must pass different tests.
   *(2014/07/possible-plausible-and-probable-big)*
8. **Stories change, and valuations must change with them.** Real-world events cause narrative
   breaks (the story ends), changes (it shifts materially), or shifts (small adjustments).
   Revisit valuations when news arrives. *(2025/01/deepseek-crashes-ai-party-story-break, 2015/08/narrative-resets-revisiting-tech-trio)*
9. **The story drives young company valuations; the numbers drive mature ones.** The balance
   shifts across the life cycle. *(2026/09/the-scaling-and-profitability-trade-off, 2024/08/the-corporate-life-cycle-corporate)*
10. **Open your story to feedback, especially from people who disagree.** Post the valuation,
    invite pushback, and update it. *(2023/02/disagreements-and-first-principles)*
11. **Runaway stories end badly.** When a story outruns any evidence and nobody checks it
    (Theranos, WeWork), you get fairy-tale pricing and a crash. *(2015/11/runaway-stories-and-fairy-tale-endings, 2019/09/runaway-story-or-meltdown-in-motion)*

## III. Intrinsic valuation mechanics

12. **Value of a business = PV of expected cash flows, discounted at a rate reflecting their risk.**
    Four questions: what are the cash flows from existing assets, what's the value of growth,
    how risky are the cash flows, and when does the firm become mature?
13. **Use cash flows to the firm (FCFF) with the cost of capital, or cash flows to equity with
    the cost of equity.** Never mix them. Consistency (currency, inflation, cash flow type,
    discount rate) beats precision.
14. **FCFF = EBIT(1 − t) − (cap ex − depreciation) − Δ non-cash working capital**, or, for
    growth companies, NOPAT − reinvestment, where reinvestment = Δ revenue / sales-to-capital.
15. **Growth isn't free.** Growth requires reinvestment, and growth adds value only when the
    return on that reinvestment (ROIC) exceeds the cost of capital. Growth with ROIC below the
    cost of capital destroys value. Sustainable growth = reinvestment rate × ROIC.
16. **The value drivers for a young or growth company:** revenue growth (how big), target
    operating margin (how profitable at scale), sales-to-capital ratio (how much reinvestment
    the growth needs), cost of capital (how risky), and failure probability (will it survive).
    This is the structure of his Ginzu spreadsheet. *(2026/04/to-trillion-dollars-and-beyond-spacex)*
17. **Terminal value rules.** Stable growth can't exceed the riskfree rate (a proxy for nominal
    growth of the economy). The cost of capital should converge toward a mature-company level.
    Reinvestment in stable growth must be consistent with growth: reinvestment rate = g / ROIC.
    Excess returns forever need a moat that lasts forever. *(2016/11/myth-55-terminal-value-ate-my-dcf)*
18. **A big terminal value isn't a flaw.** For growth companies most of the value comes from
    the terminal value, because that's when the cash flows arrive. The fix is to get the
    steady-state assumptions right, not to shorten the forecast or swap in a multiple. *(2016/11/myth-55-terminal-value-ate-my-dcf)*
19. **Deal with failure separately from the discount rate.** Young companies can fail. Estimate
    a failure probability and the proceeds if it fails, rather than padding the discount rate to
    30–60% the way VCs do. *(2013/11/valuation-myths-young-companies-cannot)*
20. **Clean up accounting before valuing.** Capitalize R&D (it's an investment, not an
    operating expense), treat leases as debt, and treat stock-based compensation as a real
    expense. Don't add SBC back to cash flows, and count options and RSUs in the share count.
    *(2014/02/stock-based-employee-compensation-value, 2018/07/share-count-confusion-dilution-employee)*
21. **Equity bridge.** Value of operating assets − debt (including leases) − minority interests
    + cash + cross-holdings and non-operating assets − value of employee options = common
    equity. Then divide by shares outstanding.
22. **Uncertainty isn't a reason not to value; it's the reason to.** Make your best estimates,
    show the distribution (simulation), and accept that you'll be wrong. Being wrong is not
    the same as being biased. *(2016/05/dcf-myth-3-you-cannot-do-valuation-when, 2013/11/value-in-eye-of-storm-why-you-should)*
23. **Young companies can be valued.** You don't need history or comparables, just a story about
    the business's future and the discipline to turn it into numbers. *(2013/11/valuation-myths-young-companies-cannot, 2026/04/to-trillion-dollars-and-beyond-spacex)*
24. **Intangibles can be valued.** Brand, people, and platforms show up in the numbers: pricing
    power shows up in margins, and network effects show up in growth and reinvestment efficiency.
    *(2023/10/invisible-yet-invaluable-valuing, 2013/10/the-brand-name-advantage-valuable)*
25. **Key persons have value, and it can be lost.** Value the company with and without the key
    person to measure their contribution. *(2023/12/the-difference-makers-key-persons)*

## IV. Risk and the cost of capital

26. **The cost of capital is an opportunity cost:** what you could earn elsewhere on an
    investment of equivalent risk. It reflects the risk of the investment, not of the entity
    making it. *(2026/02/data-update-5-for-2026-risk-and-hurdle)*
27. **Don't overthink the discount rate.** 80% of US firms have costs of capital between about
    5.3% and 9.9% in US dollars (global: about 6.3%–11.7%), as of January 2026. Time spent
    finessing it is usually time taken from the cash flows, where the real uncertainty is.
    *(2026/02/data-update-5-for-2026-risk-and-hurdle)*
28. **Riskfree rates differ by currency and must be truly riskfree.** Government bond rate −
    sovereign default spread. Since Moody's downgraded the US to Aa1 (2025), even the US dollar
    riskfree rate is the 10-year Treasury less a small default spread (0.22%). The riskfree rate
    in another currency ≈ the US riskfree rate adjusted for the inflation differential.
    *(2025/06/sovereign-ratings-default-risk-and, 2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and)*
29. **Use the implied equity risk premium, updated often.** Back the ERP out of current index
    prices and expected cash flows; don't use a historical average. Using a different ERP from
    the market's turns every company valuation into a hidden market call. Keep company
    valuations market-neutral. *(2026/03/the-price-of-risk-equity-risk-premium)*
30. **Country risk comes from where you operate, not where you're incorporated.** Country risk
    premium ≈ sovereign default spread × (equity volatility / bond volatility), a ratio of about
    1.55 in July 2026. Weight it by revenues or operations across countries. *(2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and)*
31. **Use bottom-up betas.** Average unlevered betas of comparable businesses, corrected for cash,
    relevered at your firm's debt-to-equity ratio. Regression betas are too noisy for a
    single company. *(2015/01/putting-d-in-dcf-cost-of-capital)*
32. **For private businesses with undiversified owners, use total beta.** Total beta = market
    beta / correlation with the market. This reflects all the risk the owner bears. Expect a
    higher cost of equity for the private owner than for a diversified public buyer.
33. **Cost of debt = riskfree + default spread,** from a rating or a synthetic rating based on
    interest coverage. Then apply the marginal tax rate.
34. **Debt has a tax benefit and a distress cost.** The optimal mix maximizes firm value. Most
    young firms should carry little debt. *(2026/02/data-update-7-for-2026-debt-and-taxes)*
35. **The cost of capital changes over the life cycle.** Start young firms at a high-percentile
    cost of capital and move it toward the median as they mature. For example, a small, newly
    listed global AI firm might start near 11.7% and move toward 8.65%. *(2026/02/data-update-5-for-2026-risk-and-hurdle)*

## V. Relative valuation (pricing)

36. **Pricing is a four-step process.** (1) Define the multiple consistently (EV multiples with
    operating metrics, equity multiples with equity metrics). (2) Describe its distribution
    across the market and the sector. (3) Analyze what drives it (growth, risk, margins,
    reinvestment). (4) Apply it to truly comparable firms, controlling for differences.
37. **Every multiple has fundamentals underneath.** PE is driven by growth, payout, and risk.
    EV/Sales by operating margin, growth, and reinvestment. Price-to-book by ROE minus cost of
    equity. A "cheap" multiple with weak fundamentals isn't cheap. *(2015/06/debt-cash-and-pe-why-cash-is-upper-and, 2013/07/market-multiples-global-comparison-and)*
38. **Multiples of distant revenues or users are pricing, not valuation.** User value is real
    only if user economics work: value per user, cost of acquiring users, retention. *(2017/06/usersubscriber-economics-alternative)*

## VI. The corporate life cycle

39. **Six stages:** start-up (idea), young growth, high growth, mature growth, mature stable,
    and decline. The key task shifts from building a business model, to scaling, to defending,
    to managing decline. *(2024/08/the-corporate-life-cycle-corporate)*
40. **Act your age.** Many corporate mistakes come from firms acting younger (or older) than
    they are: a declining firm buying growth, or a young firm paying dividends. Management
    should fit the stage. *(2021/12/managing-across-corporate-life-cycle, 2024/09/dealing-with-aging-daignosing-intel)*
41. **Tech companies age in dog years.** They scale faster, peak sooner, and decline faster.
    Valuations and management have to adapt. *(2015/12/aging-in-dog-years-short-glorious-life)*
42. **Decline is natural, and denial is expensive.** Value a declining firm as declining. Don't
    reinvest in a dying business at returns below the cost of capital, and don't let it buy
    growth to hide decline. *(2013/09/decline-and-denial-requiem-for)*
43. **Restraint is a virtue in mature companies.** Returning cash beats empire-building when
    good projects run out. *(2026/05/an-ode-to-restraint-lessons-from-tim)*

## VII. Startups, venture capital, and scaling

44. **VC is a pricing game.** VCs price based on what others pay and on exit multiples, with
    high target rates that bundle failure risk, illiquidity, and negotiation. That's
    rational for them but isn't a valuation of the business. *(2016/10/venture-capital-it-is-pricing-not-value, 2019/09/insights-on-vc-pricing-lessons-from)*
45. **Pre-money, post-money, play-money.** Headline post-money valuations often apply the
    latest round's price per share to all shares, ignoring the preferences and protections
    that later investors get. Unicorn pricings overstate value. *(2015/02/blood-in-shark-tank-pre-money-post, 2015/06/billion-dollar-tech-babies-blessing-of)*
46. **Scaling and profitability trade off.** Not every business should scale. The archetypes
    are lightning in a bottle, field of dreams, field of nightmares, niche star, big and broken,
    small winner, small loser, and cut your losses. Choose a path that fits the business's
    economics, not a VC's exit math. *(2026/09/the-scaling-and-profitability-trade-off)*
47. **What drives scalability:** market size and growth, industry structure, capital intensity,
    customer inertia, and key-person dependence. **What drives business building (profits):**
    unit economics, economies of scale, and moats. *(2026/09/the-scaling-and-profitability-trade-off)*
48. **Cash burn isn't bad by itself.** Burn that builds a business with good unit economics is
    investment; burn that subsidizes broken unit economics is waste. *(2016/08/the-bonfire-of-venture-capital-good-bad)*
49. **The big market delusion.** When a market is perceived as big, too many companies enter,
    each priced as if it will win, and collective overpricing follows. Bubbles are a feature of
    revolutionary change, not a bug. *(2015/08/big-markets-over-confidence-and-macro, 2019/12/the-market-is-huge-revisiting-big, 2026/08/ais-bar-mitzvah-moment-from-hype-hope)*
50. **TAM is often a gaming tool.** Bankers and founders inflate total addressable markets. Build
    the market bottom-up and be explicit about the assumptions behind it (for AI: tool versus
    replacement, which workers, which industries, which countries). *(2026/08/ais-bar-mitzvah-moment-from-hype-hope)*
51. **Big markets don't always make big businesses.** Market size, industry economics (how many
    winners, what margins), and moats together determine value. *(2026/08/ais-bar-mitzvah-moment-from-hype-hope)*
52. **Revolutionary change runs a cycle:** hope and hype, an investing build-up, business
    building, then recalibration. Investors stop pricing on promise during the business-building
    phase (the "bar mitzvah moment"). *(2026/08/ais-bar-mitzvah-moment-from-hype-hope)*
53. **ARR and run-rates are self-serving metrics** for growth businesses. Treat them as stories
    to verify, not as revenues. *(2026/08/ais-bar-mitzvah-moment-from-hype-hope)*
54. **Founders face a trade-off between control and ambition.** Scaling usually requires giving
    up control (the founder's dilemma). Know which one you value more. *(2026/09/the-scaling-and-profitability-trade-off)*

## VIII. Moats, disruption, and competition

55. **Moats show up in the numbers** as excess returns (ROIC above the cost of capital) that last.
    No moat means excess returns fade toward zero. *(2024/08/beat-your-bot-building-your-moat)*
56. **Disruption creates value for the disruptor and destroys it for the disrupted,** often more
    destruction than creation. Disruption without a viable replacement business leaves debris. *(2015/01/the-dance-of-disrupted-observations, 2026/09/the-scaling-and-profitability-trade-off)*
57. **AI is a business like any other.** Judge it on market size, unit economics, and moats, not on
    usage or capital expenditure alone. AI capex shows up as revenue for suppliers before it
    shows up as profit for builders, and markets will move before accountants write anything
    off. *(2026/08/ais-bar-mitzvah-moment-from-hype-hope, 2026/10/stock-prices-earnings-and-cashflows-ai)*
58. **Build your moat against AI** by doing what bots can't: judgment, principle-driven
    (not rule-driven) work, and personal connection. *(2024/08/beat-your-bot-building-your-moat, 2025/08/the-imitation-game-defending-against)*

## IX. Corporate finance choices

59. **Investment, financing, and dividend decisions all serve value.** Invest in projects that
    earn more than the hurdle rate, finance them with the mix that minimizes the cost of capital,
    and return cash when good projects run out.
60. **Buybacks aren't evil and dividends aren't sacred.** Both return cash. Judge them against
    the firm's investment opportunities and life-cycle stage. *(2014/09/stock-buybacks-they-are-big-they-are, 2026/02/data-update-8-for-2026-time-for)*
61. **Most acquisitions destroy value for acquirers.** That's due to overpayment, hubris,
    overstated synergy, and accounting illusions (accretion is not value). Value synergy
    explicitly and pay only for what you control. *(2012/12/acquisition-hubris-over-confident-ceos, 2012/12/acquisition-accounting-i-accretive, 2015/10/winning-at-losers-game-control-synergy)*
62. **Control has value only if things can be run differently.** The value of control = value
    run optimally − value run as is. *(2015/10/winning-at-losers-game-control-synergy)*
63. **Corporate governance matters most when management is entrenched.** Dual-class shares and
    founder worship reduce the checks on bad decisions. *(2022/11/meta-lesson-1-corporate-governance)*
64. **Holding cash (or bitcoin) is a choice with consequences.** Cash is neutral in value only if
    it earns a fair rate and investors trust management with it. *(2025/07/to-bitcoin-or-not-to-bitcoin-corporate, 2015/05/valuing-pricing-cash)*

## X. Investing

65. **There is no single best investment philosophy, only the one that fits you.** It depends on
    your beliefs about markets, your time horizon, your temperament, and your skills. *(2026/03/finding-your-investing-lodestar-in)*
66. **Value investing has struggled and needs reinvention.** Low PE and price-to-book are not the
    same as value. Intrinsic value includes growth. *(2020/10/value-investing-iii-requiem-rebirth-or)*
67. **Market timing is costly and rarely works.** The implied ERP tells you the market's price of
    risk; it's a barometer, not a timing signal. He calls himself not a market timer. *(2014/06/bubble-bubble-toil-and-trouble-costs, 2026/10/stock-prices-earnings-and-cashflows-ai)*
68. **Pass the sleep test.** Choose a portfolio you can live with through bad times, and count
    your human capital, the job that AI might disrupt, as part of your portfolio. *(2026/10/stock-prices-earnings-and-cashflows-ai)*
69. **Conviction without humility kills.** Over-conviction plus leverage turns a right-ish thesis
    into ruin. Markets can stay irrational longer than you can stay solvent. *(2026/08/the-situational-awareness-blow-up)*
70. **ESG is mostly marketing.** Goodness claims rarely translate into value or returns, and
    the measurement is inconsistent. *(2021/09/the-esg-movement-goodness-gravy-train, 2024/11/the-siren-song-of-sustainability)*
71. **Bitcoin is a currency or collectible, not an asset with cash flows.** It can be priced, not
    valued. *(2017/10/the-bitcoin-boom-asset-currency)*
72. **Data is a tool, not a truth.** Use it to test stories and check your inputs, but watch for
    the draw (false precision) and the danger (data mining and confirmation bias) of data. *(2025/01/data-update-1-for-2025-draw-and-danger, 2026/01/data-update-1-for-2026-push-and-pull-of)*

## XI. Bias and the dark side

73. **Every valuation is biased; the question is how much and in which direction.** Bias comes
    from who's paying, prior views, and the story you want to tell. Disclose it and check it. *(2016/09/fairness-opinions-fix-them-or-get-rid)*
74. **Bias and error are different.** Being wrong because the future surprised you is
    unavoidable. Being consistently wrong in one direction is bias.
75. **Complexity is often camouflage.** More inputs don't mean better valuations. Use parsimony:
    fewer, better-justified inputs. *(2016/09/keystone-kop-valuations-lazard-evercore)*
76. **Watch for banker valuations.** Fairness opinions and deal valuations tend to back into the
    price that was already agreed. *(2016/09/fairness-opinions-fix-them-or-get-rid)*
